Federal Circuit Splits Open the Door for Supreme Court Showdown Over State Betting Authority

By Staff Writer • October 3, 2026 Views:

The prediction markets industry is hurtling toward a constitutional collision course with state regulatory boards following yet another appellate defeat. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled flatly against Kalshi, delivering a definitive victory to state enforcers in Ohio and Tennessee who moved aggressively to clamp down on event contracts under state gambling statutes[cite: 7].

At the center of the dispute is a stark jurisdictional paradox: Kalshi has argued tooth and nail that its political and sports event-based financial instruments qualify as federally regulated swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act, which would override state-level oversight[cite: 7]. The Sixth Circuit dismantled that defense, ruling not only that the contracts fail to clear the statutory definition of a federal swap, but that even if they did, federal law doesn't strip states of their core police powers to police local gambling operations[cite: 7].

This decision deepens an unmanageable fracture across federal jurisdictions. With the Ninth Circuit previously siding against prediction platforms while the Third Circuit carved out a friendly precedent protecting them in New Jersey, the legal map is hopelessly fractured[cite: 7].

With conflicting appellate rulings tearing through the industry, lower court maneuvering has reached a dead end. Legal teams are preparing petitions for certiorari to force the Supreme Court's hand, leaving prediction markets hanging on whether the high court will step in to settle who ultimately wears the badge over digital wagering[cite: 7].
